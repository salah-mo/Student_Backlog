# W01-01: Read "Modern Software Engineering" Ch. 1 — Introduction, and Write Your Summary

> **ID:** W01-01 | **Week:** 1 | **Type:** Book Reading | **Book:** _Modern Software Engineering_ — Dave Farley | **Pages:** 3–9

## Objective

This is your first reading task from the book _Modern Software Engineering_ by Dave Farley. In pages 3–9 of Chapter 1, the author explains what engineering really means — the practical application of science — and introduces the two big ideas the whole book is built on: learning and managing complexity. Understanding this chapter matters because every later task in this program assumes these ideas. You will show your understanding by summarizing the text, recalling its key lists from memory, and answering the question the chapter raises: what is the difference between a programmer and an engineer?

## Requirements

### 1. Read the assigned pages

Read pages 3–9 of Chapter 1 ("Introduction"). While reading, pay attention to these topics:

- Engineering as the practical application of science, and the scientific method (Characterize → Hypothesize → Predict → Experiment)
- The working definition of software engineering given in the book
- The five learning techniques
- The five tools for dealing with complexity
- How the term "software engineering" was born (Margaret Hamilton, NATO conference, 1968)
- Fred Brooks' idea of "No Silver Bullet"

### 2. Write a 3-sentence summary

- Summarize the assigned pages in **exactly 3 sentences**, in your own words.
- A good summary captures the main idea of the chapter section, not a list of every detail.

### 3. Recall the key lists from memory

- With the **book closed**, list the five learning techniques and the five tools for dealing with complexity.
- Add one short sentence of your own under each item explaining what it means.
- After writing your lists, open the book and check yourself. If you missed or misremembered something, mark it and add the correct item — this tells you what to review.

### 4. Answer: Programmer vs Engineer

- In your own words, answer the question: **what is the difference between a programmer and an engineer?**
- Base your answer on the ideas in the chapter (about 2–5 sentences). An answer that only gives your opinion, without connecting to the reading, is not enough.

## Deliverables

- A written summary document in your repository (for example, `reading/week1-ch1-summary.md`) containing:
  - The 3-sentence summary
  - The two lists from memory: the 5 learning techniques and the 5 complexity tools, each with a short explanation
  - Your answer to "Programmer vs Engineer"

## Acceptance Criteria

- The summary is exactly 3 sentences and reflects the main ideas of pages 3–9.
- The 5 learning techniques and the 5 complexity tools are listed from memory, each with a short explanation in your own words.
- The "Programmer vs Engineer" question is answered in your own words, using ideas from the chapter.
- Everything is written in English, in your own words — no copied sentences from the book.

## Constraints

- Do the recall exercise (Requirement 3) with the book closed. Re-reading the pages as many times as you need **before** writing is allowed and encouraged.
- This is a reading-and-writing task: no code, no tools, nothing to install.
- Follow the AI usage agreement you wrote in W0-3: the summary, the lists, and the answer must be your own writing. You may ask AI only to explain concepts you did not understand while reading.
